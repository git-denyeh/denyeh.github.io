Bless Your
Oblivious Heart

You know who you are. Actually — that's the problem. You don't.

This is for everyone who's sat through a green light because the person ahead was scrolling Instagram. For everyone stuck behind someone doing 47 in the left lane on the highway, wondering if they're lost or just indifferent to the world around them. For everyone who's watched a grown adult leave a cart three feet from the return and walk away like they just solved a problem.

You're not a bad person. You're just oblivious — and oblivious has consequences. It causes accidents. It wastes people's time. It makes the world just a little harder for everyone around you.

If you're reading this and nodding — welcome. You're among friends.
If you're reading this and getting defensive — bless your heart. Pull up a chair. This one's for you.

October 19, 2026#

Unexpected item

Somebody put it about as plainly as it can be put. We replaced the cashiers with self-checkout, then hired people to stand there and supervise the self-checkout.

So there's still an employee. She just doesn't get to ring anything up. She gets to walk over with her little badge every time the machine has a feeling.

And the machine has a lot of feelings. Unexpected item in the bagging area. There is no unexpected item. It's the bread. I scanned it. You watched me scan it. You beeped about it.

Meanwhile the man in front of me is running a full week of groceries for six through a lane built for a sandwich and a Gatorade. He's weighing bananas like he's defusing something. He has coupons. Paper ones.

Nobody planned this. The store wanted to save on payroll. We wanted to skip a line. Everybody got what they asked for, and now we all stand in a new line, waiting on the same one person, who is doing her job and ours both.

I'm not mad. I'm just going back to the register with the teenager who never looks up. At least he knows what a banana weighs.

October 12, 2026#

Aisle officials

Somebody suggested grocery stores hire aisle officials. You stand in the middle of the aisle visiting with an old friend, you get a fine. Five dollars, and it goes up every time you do it.

I'm not saying I'd vote for it. I'm saying I'd want to see the ballot.

I can picture the official, too. Striped shirt, little whistle, standing by the canned tomatoes. Not mean about it. Just a polite tweet and a hand pointing toward the end cap.

I don't mind people visiting. It's one of the better things about living somewhere folks know each other. Catching up by the bread is practically a sacrament around here. Just do it over to the side, where the rest of us aren't trying to get to the milk.

Five dollars seems fair for a first offense. By the third one you've paid for somebody else's groceries, and honestly that might be the most neighborly part of the whole idea.

October 5, 2026#

Committee in aisle seven

You've seen it. Five grown adults standing shoulder to shoulder in the cereal aisle, deciding on cereal. As a group. Like it's a zoning hearing.

Nobody's in charge, and nobody's moving. The rest of us stand there with our carts, holding what we hope is a polite expression.

I don't need anybody to hurry. I just need one of the five to notice there's a world on the other side of the Cheerios. One of them. Any of them. You can keep the meeting going, just scoot it over a little.

Eventually you go around by way of the bread. Takes an extra minute. Last I checked, they were still deliberating.

October 5, 2026#

Yesterday's bill

Here's a story I keep hearing in one form or another. Fella ignores the insurance agent for a month. Calls go unanswered, letters go in the pile. Then he gets hit by a car and suddenly he's real interested in getting covered. Starting yesterday, if possible.

I don't laugh at the getting hit part. Nobody deserves that. But the timing is something.

Insurance is about the only thing in life you have to buy before you know you need it. That's the whole deal. The agent wasn't bothering you. He was trying to get there before the car did.

October 3, 2026#

They had to pass a law

Connecticut put a left-lane camping law on the books for October 1. You can't sit in the left lane unless you're passing, taking an exit, or getting around something in the way.

They had to write that down. That's the part that gets me. The passing lane has been the passing lane my whole life. Somewhere along the way enough people decided it was their living room that a legislature had to remind them.

Oblivious isn't a personality. It's a traffic pattern.

October 3, 2026#

The comments are the post

A Florida left-lane clip is going around. I don't even need the video. You already know it. Someone planted in the left lane, a line of cars behind them, and a comment section full of people who have lived this exact Tuesday.

The funny part isn't the driver. It's how many of us can describe them without watching.
