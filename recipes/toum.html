Toum

Lebanese whipped garlic sauce

The pure garlic intensity held in a stable, fluffy emulsion of oil and acid. Nothing else comes close for pita and shawarma.

Method

  1. Place the peeled garlic cloves and salt in a food processor. Pulse until finely minced, scraping down once. Add 1 Tbsp lemon juice and the ice cube. Process 20–30 seconds until a smooth, sticky paste forms. Why this works: Salt draws moisture and the ice keeps the mixture cold so the emulsion doesn’t break later.
  2. With the processor running on high, begin adding the oil in a slow, thin, steady stream (start with just a few drops, then a thin thread). After about ⅓ of the oil is in, the mixture should look white and begin to thicken.
  3. Alternate the remaining oil with the rest of the lemon juice in three additions, always keeping the stream slow. Process until the sauce is bright white, thick, and holds soft peaks — like a very stiff aioli. Total oil incorporation usually takes 3–4 minutes. Why this works: Slow addition + cold temperature creates a stable oil-in-water emulsion. Rushing this step is the #1 reason toum separates.
  4. Taste. It should be aggressively garlicky and bright. Adjust with a pinch more salt or a few drops of lemon if needed. Transfer to a jar.

Tips

  • Emulsion insurance: If it starts to look oily or thin at any point, stop adding oil, throw in another ice cube or ½ tsp lemon juice, and process hard until it pulls back together.
  • Make-ahead: Keeps 2–3 weeks refrigerated in a sealed jar. The garlic mellows slightly after day one but stays potent.
  • Shawarma application: Spread a thick layer on the pita first, then pile the chicken and vegetables. The sauce acts as both flavor bomb and moisture barrier so the bread doesn’t get soggy.

Variations

  • Spicy toum: Blend in ½–1 tsp Aleppo pepper or a small seeded bird’s-eye chili with the garlic.
  • Herb lift: A few leaves of fresh mint pulsed in at the end for a brighter finish.
