Brown Basmati Lebanese-Style Rice

Plain Lebanese-style rice made with brown basmati for extra fiber and nutty depth. Cooked in light chicken stock and samneh (clarified butter) so it stays pure white in appearance while carrying real savory flavor—no vermicelli, no visible add-ins.

Method

  1. Rinse the brown basmati thoroughly under cold water until it runs mostly clear. Soak for 30 minutes, then drain well. (Soaking shortens cooking time and improves texture.)
  2. In a heavy pot with a tight-fitting lid, melt the samneh over medium heat. Add the drained rice and stir for 2–3 minutes until every grain is coated and lightly toasted. This step carries the flavor and helps the grains stay separate.
  3. Pour in the hot stock and salt. Stir once, bring to a full boil.
  4. Cover tightly, reduce heat to the lowest possible setting, and cook undisturbed for 40–45 minutes. Do not lift the lid.
  5. Turn off the heat and leave the lid on for 10–12 minutes so residual steam finishes the rice.
  6. Fluff gently with a fork. The grains should be tender, separate, and lightly savory.

Tips

  • Brown basmati needs more liquid and time than white. Start with 4 cups stock; if still firm after 40 minutes, add 2–3 Tbsp more hot stock, cover, and give it another 5 minutes.
  • Samneh (or good ghee) is the key to restaurant-depth flavor. Regular butter works but is milder.
  • Pairs the same way as the classic white version—under kabobs, kafta, stews, or with a spoonful of yogurt.
    Why this works: Toasting the rice in fat coats each grain and develops nutty flavor. Absorption cooking + long rest produces light, separate grains instead of sticky porridge. Light stock + samneh gives savory depth while keeping the rice pure and unadorned.
