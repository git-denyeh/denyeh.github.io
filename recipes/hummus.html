Extra-Garlicky Hummus

Garlic first, apologies never

Raw cloves for sharp bite, a touch of roasted for depth, and enough lemon and tahini to keep the emulsion silky rather than harsh.

Method

  1. Roast the garlic head if needed: cut the top off, drizzle with a little oil, wrap in foil, and roast at 400°F / 200°C for 35–40 minutes until soft and golden. Squeeze out the cloves. Why this works: Roasting converts harsh alliin compounds into sweeter, deeper notes so you can push the total garlic higher without bitterness.
  2. In a food processor, combine the raw garlic cloves, roasted garlic, salt, lemon juice, and cumin. Pulse until a rough paste forms, scraping once. Let it sit 2–3 minutes. Why this works: Acid and salt begin breaking down the raw garlic enzymes so the final hummus isn’t sharp in a one-dimensional way.
  3. Add the tahini and process 30–45 seconds until thick and creamy. Scrape down. With the machine running, stream in the olive oil.
  4. Add the drained chickpeas and process 1–2 full minutes, stopping to scrape. The mixture will look stiff. Now drizzle in ice-cold water 1 tablespoon at a time while processing until the hummus is light, fluffy, and holds soft peaks. Taste and adjust salt or lemon. Why this works: Prolonged processing + cold water creates a stable, airy emulsion. Warm water or under-processing leaves it pasty.
  5. Transfer to a shallow bowl. Make a well with the back of a spoon, flood with olive oil, and dust with paprika. Serve immediately or chill.

Tips

  • Garlic intensity control: 5 raw + roasted is properly aggressive. For true garlic-forward (almost Toum-adjacent), go to 7–8 raw cloves and increase lemon slightly.
  • Make-ahead: Keeps 4–5 days refrigerated. The garlic softens and integrates overnight—often even better the next day.
  • Texture insurance: If it ever seizes or looks grainy, keep processing and add another tablespoon of ice water + a teaspoon of tahini. It will come back.

Variations

  • Toum-boosted: Fold 2–3 tablespoons of your Lebanese Toum into the finished hummus for an even more explosive garlic hit and lighter texture.
  • Next-level: Warm the chickpeas slightly before blending and finish with a splash of the chickpea canning liquid (aquafaba) instead of water for extra silk.
