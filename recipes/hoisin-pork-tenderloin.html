Hoisin-Glazed Pork Tenderloin

With broccoli over basmati rice

High-heat sear builds a savory crust on the tenderloin while the hoisin glaze turns glossy and clinging; quick-steamed broccoli stays bright and crisp-tender, balancing the sauce’s sweetness with clean bitterness.

Method

  1. Rinse the basmati until the water runs mostly clear. Cook by absorption (or your preferred method) until fluffy and keep warm. Rinsing removes excess surface starch so the grains stay separate.
  2. Pat the pork medallions dry and season both sides with salt and pepper. Heat a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat with 1–2 tbsp oil until shimmering.
  3. Sear the pork in a single layer (batches if needed) for 1½–2 minutes per side until golden but still slightly pink inside. Transfer to a plate. Dry surface + high heat drives the Maillard reaction for maximum flavor; pull early so it doesn’t dry out.
  4. Add the remaining oil if the pan looks dry. Stir-fry the broccoli for 1–2 minutes until the edges start to color. Splash in 2–3 tbsp water, cover briefly (or keep tossing) for 60–90 seconds until crisp-tender and bright green, then uncover and let any remaining liquid evaporate. The short steam finishes the denser florets without turning them soft.
  5. Add the garlic and ginger; toss 20–30 seconds until fragrant.
  6. Return the pork and any juices to the pan. Pour in the hoisin, soy sauce, vinegar or lime, sesame oil, and chili if using. Stir to coat, then add the cornstarch slurry. Cook 1–2 minutes, tossing constantly, until the glaze thickens and clings to everything. The slurry gives the sauce body so it coats instead of pooling.
  7. Taste and adjust seasoning. Serve immediately over the basmati rice.

Tips

  • Target 140–145 °F internal on the pork; carryover heat finishes it. A 2–3 minute rest keeps it juicy.
  • Cut broccoli small and uniform so it cooks evenly in the short window.
  • Sauce base and slurry can be mixed ahead. Leftovers reheat best in a hot skillet with a splash of water to revive the glaze.

Variations

  • Add a light dusting of Chinese five-spice to the pork before searing for deeper aroma.
  • Finish with toasted sesame seeds and a squeeze of lime, or a drizzle of chili crisp for heat and texture.
