Ragù alla Bolognese

The slow, traditional one

The slow one. Three hours on low, milk to calm the tomato, and it's better tomorrow.

Makes
6 servings
Prep

Cook

3 hr 20 min

Scale

Ingredients

Quantities scaled. Times, temperatures and pan sizes don't scale; trust your eyes.

Method

  1. In a heavy pot, gently cook the pancetta in the oil until it renders. Add the soffritto and sweat over low heat 5–8 minutes without browning.
  2. Raise the heat, add the ground beef, and cook 8–10 minutes, breaking it up, until it sizzles.
  3. Pour in the wine and let it evaporate completely.
  4. Stir in the tomato paste and passata. Add a cup of hot broth, cover, and simmer very gently 2–3 hours, adding more hot broth or water as it thickens.
  5. In the last 15–20 minutes, stir in the milk to mellow the tomato acidity. Season with salt and pepper.
  6. Serve with fresh tagliatelle (traditional), or rigatoni or pappardelle. A little cream is sometimes added for dried pasta, never for tagliatelle.

Tips

  • This is the recipe deposited by the Bologna delegation of the Accademia Italiana della Cucina in 1982 — the closest thing to an official version.
  • Milk, not cream, is what softens the acidity. Long, slow cooking is what makes it rich.
  • Freezes beautifully; tastes even better the next day.

Based on Accademia Italiana della Cucina (registered 1982)
